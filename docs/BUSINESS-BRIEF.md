## What the product is

A bedtime scripture ritual for families with children aged 1–5. LDS in voice,
broadly Christian in substance. One short story every night, plus the things
that make the night actually happen.

**The nightly rhythm**
- **The Morning Lantern** — the devotional, sent every morning so a parent has
  all day to think before bedtime arrives. Written at **four depths**: a quick
  read of a few minutes through to a full study with word studies, historical
  background, General Conference talks and commentary.
- **The bedtime story** — a short illustrated scripture story, written for
  ages 1–5, with a Feeling Moment, a Remember This, and a parent prompt.
- **A lullaby** for that night, with words and music, plus one signature
  lullaby that is always there.
- **Narration** for the nights when reading aloud is too much.

**The things that make people stay**
- **The Virtue Library** — every story tagged by 36 virtues, and findable five
  ways: by virtue, by life situation, by feeling, by traveler, by scripture.
  This answers *"my child is frightened tonight — what do I read?"*
- **Family Stories** — the answers a parent writes after a night's story,
  tagged the same way, kept as the family's own history and attributable to
  each child by name.
- **A personal journal**, shared between the app and the website.
- **The Sacred World Timeline** — a pan-and-zoom timeline, 4000 BC to 2030,
  with 88 events across 33 layers: gospel, empires, wars, inventions, world
  faiths, prophecy-and-fulfilment, and the family's own ancestors placed at
  their real moment in history.
- **Four illustrated books** already written (the Old Testament series, 91
  stories), with more volumes planned.

**The editorial rule, and it matters:** everything on the timeline is marked
*confirmed*, *believed*, or *debated*. Nothing is asserted that the evidence
does not support. Levi is firm on this.

## The pricing decision, and the reasoning

**Now: $6.99/month · $49/year**, 7-day free trial. Yearly is the default
everywhere — shown first, larger, marked *Best value*, with *"just $4.08 a
month"* beside it. Previously $10.99/$98.91.

Why:
- **$49/year is the number that matters.** Annual subscribers are twelve
  months you don't have to re-win, and a daily-content product lives or dies
  on churn. Front-loaded cash also funds the year of content being promised.
- **The margin advantage is real.** Checkout is on Payhip, on his own site,
  not through the App Store — so he keeps far more per dollar than the
  competitors do, and can charge less while netting more.
- **Not going lower.** $5.99 saves a subscriber $12 a year and persuades
  nobody who said no at $6.99, while moving the product into throwaway-app
  territory. Prices can go up later; they cannot come down twice.

**Founding Family offer — decided, not yet live.** First 500 families at
$39/year, locked for as long as they stay. Built into the website and waiting
on one Payhip product being created. The point is scarcity and rewarding the
people who subscribe before there are any reviews.

**Outstanding and only Levi can do it:** change the two Payhip product prices
(the website says $6.99/$49; Payhip still charges the old prices until he
does), create the Founding Family product, and move existing subscribers down
to the new price so early supporters never pay more than newcomers.

## The competition (checked August 2026)

| | Price | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| Minno | $10.99/mo · $69.99/yr | Christian kids streaming — VeggieTales, Superbook |
| Hallow | $9.99/mo · $69.99/yr | Catholic prayer and meditation, audio-first, adults |
| Bible App for Kids | Free | Animated stories and games, ages 4–8 |
| Gospel Library | Free | The Church's own — complete, but a reference library |

At $49 the Lantern is $21/year under both paid competitors.

**Nobody combines what this does.** Minno is video. Hallow is prayer for
adults. The free ones are games for children with nothing for the parent.
None of them have a written book series, a devotional written for the
grown-up at four depths, family journaling that becomes family history,
lullabies with music, and a history timeline.

**But breadth is why people stay, not why they join.** A homepage that lists
everything reads as complicated. The pitch should be one promise — *ten quiet
minutes, one scripture story, every night* — and let the rest be discovered in
week two, so it feels like more than was paid for. That feeling is what makes
somebody tell their ward.

## The real problem to work on

Not the product. The product is further along than the marketing.

**Getting the first 500 families.** Word of mouth inside wards is the obvious
channel and nothing has been built to make that easy — no referral, no way for
a family who loves it to hand it to another family. There are also no reviews
yet, which is exactly what the Founding Family offer is meant to work around.

Good questions for a strategy conversation:
- How does one ward family tell another, in a way that takes ten seconds?
- What is the single sentence on the homepage?
- Is there a free tier or sampler that seeds word of mouth without cannibalising?
- What does the first 90 days of marketing actually look like, week by week?
