--------------------------------------------------
BRAND RULES (hard rules)
--------------------------------------------------
1. Say "Heavenly Father," never bare "God," in all character dialogue and story-facing narration. Allowed: lowercase "the false god Baal" type phrases, and the Narrator may say "the Lord."
2. Scripture characters speak ONLY words that are in the scripture text (King James Version for the Bible; the Book of Mormon / Doctrine and Covenants text for those books), lightly shortened if needed but never changed in meaning. If the scripture gives a person no words, they get no lines; the Narrator describes them instead.
3. Jesus Christ: reverent always. He speaks only His recorded words, calmly. No jokes near Him, no Traveler humor during His scenes. Shown in soft, warm light, kind face, traditional white robe. Heavenly Father is never shown as a figure; His presence is light and/or a voice speaking only scripture words.
4. The Travelers NEVER interact with scripture characters. Scripture characters never see or hear them. The Travelers watch from nearby (behind a rock, on a hill, in the crowd's edge), slightly glowing in their trait colors so kids always know who is a visitor.
5. Do not add events, miracles, names or dialogue that are not in scripture. Small sensory details (wind, sounds, dust, the color of the sky) are fine. If something is commonly assumed but not in the text, leave it out.
6. No ending where the Narrator tells the child to whisper or say something. End with a warm statement, not an assignment.
7. Humor lives in the playroom and in the Travelers' reactions during calm story moments, never during sacred moments (prayers, visions, miracles, the Savior, death, suffering).
8. No copyrighted characters, songs, brands or show names in any prompt. Never write "in the style of" any studio or show. Use the STYLE LOCK below instead.
9. Fear level: G-rated. Dangers (lions, giants, storms, fire) are shown as tense but safe; no blood, no wounds, no cruelty shown up close. Cut away and let the Narrator carry it.
10. Audience: if Latter-day Saint, Book of Mormon and Restoration language is welcome. If Christian, keep to doctrine shared across Christians. If both, keep the lesson and wording common to both.

THE CHANT (every episode, word for word)
--------------------------------------------------
LAUNCH (all four, in unison):
"Brave hearts, bright sparks, kind hands, wise eyes — to the world's most treasured true stories we fly!"
Gestures (kids at home copy these):
- "Brave hearts" — both hands crossed flat over the heart, then two quick heart-beat taps (thump-thump).
- "Bright sparks" — fingertips touch the temples, then burst open beside the head like fireworks (Scout adds a tiny "pop!").
- "Kind hands" — hands open palms-up and reach forward like giving a gift, then a little wave.
- "Wise eyes" — make circle "binoculars" with both hands around the eyes and lean in.
- "…we fly!" — arms shoot up, all four jump into the projection.

RETURN (all four, in unison, as the story ends):
"Treasure in our hearts, light to lead the way — home we fly to live it every day!"
Gestures: hands cupped over the heart (treasure), one hand points forward like holding a lantern (light to lead the way), arms up and jump on "home we fly."

EPISODE STRUCTURE (target 7:00–9:00)
--------------------------------------------------
SCENE 1 — THEME OPEN (0:20–0:40) [REUSE every episode]
Theme song plays over the four Travelers' intro shots and the logo.

SCENE 2 — PLAYROOM: THE PROBLEM (1:00–1:30)
The four are playing in the playroom/library (a game with real kid logic: blanket fort, pretend grocery store, a block tower contest). Through the play, the STAR TRAVELER runs into a real-life kid problem that matches tonight's scripture lesson (being scared, being left out, wanting to quit, telling the truth, sharing, forgiving a sibling, being patient). Show the feeling in their face and body. Keep it light and funny first, then one honest beat. Include one joke for kids (physical/silly) and one for parents (dry, knowing).
Apply the Five Stones lightly through the scene: SPOT the feeling, NAME it in kid words.

SCENE 3 — THE TRAVEL CHEST OPENS (0:20–0:30) [REUSE most shots; only the final page shot is new]
Start from the uploaded chest picture that matches Scene 2's time of day (day, sunset, dusk or night; default night for bedtime). In its image prompt, tell ChatGPT to change ONLY the word on the open book's page to tonight's book name (it currently says "Esther") and keep everything else identical. The glowing white space above the chest is where the projection of tonight's story appears.
The chest lid creaks open on its own, golden light spills out, the scripture book rises, pages flutter and land on tonight's story. A light grows from the page and a glowing projection of the story's setting appears in the air.

SCENE 4 — THE LAUNCH CHANT (0:15–0:20) [REUSE]
The four line up and do the chant with gestures.

SCENE 5 — FLY-THROUGH (0:08–0:12) [REUSE the tunnel; swap the arrival shot]
They jump into the projection, swirl through a gold-and-teal light tunnel up into the uploaded starry sky background (navy sky, guiding gold star, golden clouds), and land softly at the edge of the scripture scene.

SCENE 6 — THE SCRIPTURE STORY (3:00–4:00)
Tell the story in 4–6 BEATS in scripture order. Mix: Narrator (warm, storyteller), scripture characters (their scripture words only), and the Travelers' hushed reactions to each other. The STAR TRAVELER says their catchphrase at least once here, at a moment that fits. Each Traveler reacts in character (Bo wants to help, Scout notices details, Willow feels for people, Wren asks the meaningful question). One sacred beat is fully reverent: no Traveler jokes, music drops to soft strings or silence. The STAR TRAVELER visibly connects the story to their own problem (a look, a quiet line).
Five Stones: FIND the truth in the story.

SCENE 7 — RETURN CHANT + TRIP HOME (0:15–0:20) [REUSE the tunnel]
As the last story image glows, they do the Return chant and fly back.

SCENE 8 — PLAYROOM: WHAT WE LEARNED (0:50–1:10)
They land in a heap (small gag). They talk it through like real kids. The STAR TRAVELER makes a clear, specific decision (Five Stones: FORK — the choice). A family grown-up may join for one warm line. End with CALM: a settled, happy moment.

SCENE 9 — THE NEXT DAY (0:40–1:00)
Show the STAR TRAVELER actually doing it the next day in a real setting (school, park, kitchen, church, the neighbor's yard). Short montage, few or no words, set to TONIGHT'S LANTERN SONG. It should cost them something small, and it should work out in a believable way (not magic).

SCENE 10 — GOODNIGHT (0:30–0:45)
Evening, lantern glow, the four (or the star Traveler with family) getting cozy. The Narrator gives one warm closing line tying the scripture to the choice. The BEDTIME LULLABY plays over a slow push-in on the travel chest and the end card. [END CARD REUSE: the wide sky background + logo + the book cover for tonight's volume (Old Testament, New Testament, Book of Mormon or Doctrine and Covenants) + the Lantern icon for the Lantern subscription]

OPTIONAL: once every few episodes, add a 20–30 second sing-along (a short repeating chorus with simple actions) in Scene 8 or 10. Mark it SING-ALONG and write lyrics that are original, 4–8 short lines.

--------------------------------------------------
PRODUCTION RULES (so the shots stitch together)
--------------------------------------------------
- Every shot is 6, 10 or 15 seconds (the video tool's clip lengths). Most shots: 6 seconds. Dialogue-heavy shots: 10. Big moments: 15.
- Total shots: aim for 45–60. Shots marked REUSE are built once and reused.
- Each shot has ONE camera move and ONE main action. Simple motion animates best.
- Continuity: for shots that continue the same action, write "START FROM LAST FRAME OF [shot ID]". For new angles write "CUT." Use "DISSOLVE" only for magic and time changes.
- Keep lighting, time of day, wardrobe and prop positions consistent within a scene. List them in a Scene Bible line at the top of each scene.
- Video prompts never ask the video tool to speak lines. Voices are added separately. Ask for "mouth moving as if talking" when a character speaks, and "no music, no speech; ambient sound only" in every video prompt.
- No new on-screen text inside any generated image or video (the playroom's existing signs are fine). Titles and captions are added in CapCut.
- Shot IDs: S[scene]-[shot], for example S06-04. Files: EP[##]_S06-04.png, EP[##]_S06-04.mp4, EP[##]_VO_[line#]_[SPEAKER].mp3.

--------------------------------------------------
WHAT TO DELIVER (in this exact order)
--------------------------------------------------
A. EPISODE CARD: title (kid-catchy, under 8 words), scripture reference, the one-sentence lesson, star Traveler, the real-life problem, target runtime, audience.

B. SCRIPTURE CHECK: list every scripture quote you used with its exact reference (book chapter:verse). Mark anything you shortened. Confirm no added events.

C. FULL SCRIPT: screenplay style, scene by scene. Each line: line number, SPEAKER, (delivery note), line. Delivery notes use simple tags a voice tool understands, for example [laughs], [whispers], [excited], [gently], [pause]. Narrator lines short (1–3 sentences).

D. SHOT LIST: a table per scene with columns: Shot ID | Seconds | Continuity (START FROM LAST FRAME / CUT / DISSOLVE / REUSE) | IMAGE PROMPT (for ChatGPT; begins with the STYLE LOCK; describes who is where, faces, pose, setting, lighting, camera angle) | VIDEO PROMPT (for Grok Imagine image-to-video; one camera move, one action, character motion, mouth movement if talking, "no music, no speech; ambient sound only") | Lines spoken in this shot (line numbers) | Music cue | Sound effects.

E. VOICE CAST SHEET: for each speaker (Narrator, 4 Travelers, each scripture character, each family member): a voice description I can paste into a voice designer (age, warmth, pace, accent, energy), plus a backup choice if voice design is unavailable (a stock voice type + pitch/speed settings). Keep the four Travelers' voices the same every episode.

F. VOICE LINE LIST: every line in order as a numbered list with file name, speaker and the exact text with tags, ready to paste one at a time into ElevenLabs or CapCut text-to-speech.

G. MUSIC + SOUND CUE SHEET: timeline of when the Theme song, background scores (describe mood, instruments, tempo), Lantern song, sing-along and Lullaby play, with fade in/out points; plus a list of every sound effect with shot ID.

H. CAPCUT ASSEMBLY ORDER: the track layout (video, voices, music, SFX, captions) and a numbered build order.

I. YOUTUBE PACKAGE: 3 title options, a description (with scripture reference and a gentle invite to the Lantern subscription), 10 tags, one thumbnail image prompt (bright, one big emotional face, the scripture setting behind, no text in the image; add text in CapCut), and a 30–60 second vertical Shorts cut list using existing shots.

J. GUARDRAIL REPORT (do this last, then act on it):
Score the whole kit 1–10 on each line below. Be strict, like a critic who wants it to fail.
 1. Scripture accuracy (quotes, order of events, nothing added) — HARD GATE: must be 10.
 2. Reverence and doctrine (Savior, Heavenly Father, sacred beats) — HARD GATE: must be 10.
 3. Brand rules (names, "Heavenly Father," catchphrase used by the star, chants exact, gestures included, no ending assignment) — HARD GATE: must be 10.
 4. Structure complete (all 10 scenes, runtime adds up to 7:00–9:00 — show the math).
 5. Character truth (each Traveler acts from their trait every scene).
 6. Kid fun (laughs, movement, wonder, something to copy along with).
 7. Parent appeal (at least two jokes or moments that land for adults).
 8. Lesson clarity (problem → scripture truth → specific choice → follow-through, all obvious to a 5-year-old).

Show me only the FINAL version of every section plus a short change log (what you fixed each round) and the final scorecard. Rules for you while working: Never guess a scripture detail — if my book pages and the scripture text disagree, follow the scripture and tell me where my book differs. If something essential is missing (for example the star Traveler), ask me one short question before starting.

## 2. Fresh-eyes Guardrail Check

```text
You are a strict reviewer for "Little Scripture Travelers," an animated scripture series for children 3–9. Below is a complete episode kit written by someone else. Your job is to find what is wrong, not to praise it.

Check against these rules:
- Scripture: every quoted line matches the King James Bible or Book of Mormon / D&C text (check each reference); events are in scripture order; nothing added.
- Reverence: Jesus Christ speaks only recorded words, no jokes near sacred moments; Heavenly Father never shown as a figure.
- Brand: characters are Beau "Bo the Brave" (brown, courage, "Big heart, brave start!"), Scout "Scout the Seeker" (green, curiosity, "Look and see — what could it be?"), Willow "Willow the Warm" (purple, compassion, "Warm hugs, warm hearts!"), Wren "Wren the Wonderer" (coral red, wisdom, "Pause and ponder — what a wonder!"). Says "Heavenly Father," never bare "God." The star Traveler uses their catchphrase. Return chant is exactly "Treasure in our hearts, light to lead the way — home we fly to live it every day!" Travelers never interact with scripture characters. No ending that tells the child to whisper or say something.
- Structure: 10 scenes; runtime adds up to 7:00–9:00 (redo the math yourself).
- Buildability: every shot is 6, 10 or 15 seconds; one action per shot; every image prompt starts with the style lock and has no text in the image; no named shows, studios or brands; every video prompt says no music and no speech.
- Fun and lesson: a 5-year-old could say what the problem was, what the story taught, and what the star Traveler did the next day.

Output:
1. A scorecard 1–10 for: Scripture, Reverence, Brand, Structure, Character truth, Kid fun, Parent appeal, Lesson clarity, Continuity, Buildability.
