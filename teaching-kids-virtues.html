✦ TEACHING KIDS VIRTUES

Teaching kids virtues, one scripture story at a time

When your child is struggling to share, to tell the truth or to be brave, a story teaches what a lecture can't. The Virtue Library sorts every Little Scripture Travelers story by the virtue it teaches, so you can find the right one tonight.

How to use a story to teach a virtue

  • Pick the virtue your child needs today, like patience after a hard afternoon or courage before a first day.
  • Read the story at bedtime, when they are calm and listening.
  • Ask one question: "What did they do that was brave?"
  • Tell your own story of a time you needed the same virtue.

Below are the 36 virtues in the Virtue Library, with a few of the stories that teach each one. Subscribers can search all of them by virtue, feeling or situation in the Virtue Library.

Honesty & Truth-Telling

Telling the truth even when a lie would be easier, and letting your words match what really happened. (16 stories)

  • Where Are You? · Genesis 3:8–13
    Reach for this when your child wants to hide after a mistake and needs to know God keeps asking, "Where are you?" with love.
  • Micaiah Tells the Truth · 1 Kings 22
    Reach for this when your child is tempted to say what others want to hear instead of what's true.
  • The King Saves the People · Esther
    Reach for this when your child is scared to tell the truth but the truth could protect someone they love.
  • A Good Name · Proverbs 22
    Reach for this when nobody would have known — and your child chose right anyway, or did not.

Obedience

Choosing to follow Heavenly Father and the people He has given to guide you — the first time, and with a willing heart. (62 stories)

  • Adam Is Baptized · Moses 6:52, 57–58, 64–68
    Reach for this when your child is learning about baptism and needs to see how following Jesus brings a clean, quiet peace.
  • The Lord Lived with Them · Moses 7:16–17
    Reach for this when your child is choosing the right and needs to know the Lord stays near those who do.
  • Noah, a Good Man · Genesis 6:8–9; Moses 8:13–27
    Reach for this when your child feels alone in choosing right and needs to see Noah keep walking with Heavenly Father.
  • Build a Big Boat · Genesis 6:13–22
    Reach for this when your child is facing a big job and needs to learn to listen, obey, and build it one step at a time.

Heeding Wise Counsel

Listening to parents, prophets, and teachers who can see further down the road than you can. (15 stories)

  • Angels Lead Lot Out · Genesis 19:15–17, 26
    Reach for this when your child hesitates to follow and needs the picture of angels leading Lot out by the hand.
  • A Prophet Like Moses · Deuteronomy 18:15, 18; Acts 3:22; 3 Nephi 20:23
    Reach for this when your child needs help listening to a leader and needs the picture of Jesus, the Prophet Moses promised.
  • Two Kings, One Choice · 1 Kings 12
    Reach for this when your child needs to hear why listening to wise parents matters more than listening to peers.
  • Elijah Goes Up to Heaven · 2 Kings 2
    Reach for this when a friendship or family bond is changing and your child needs comfort that loving loyalty carries on.

Self-Control

Feeling a strong feeling all the way through without letting it choose your hands or your words for you. (24 stories)

  • Quiet Like a Lamb · Isaiah 53:7; Mark 14:53–61
    Reach for this when your child is wound up or being unfairly blamed and needs a picture of staying quiet and kind.
  • Water at Meribah · Numbers 20:2–12
    Reach for this when your child gets hot and does things their own way, and needs the picture of Moses hitting the rock.
  • Saul Gets Jealous of David · 1 Samuel 18:6-16
    Reach for this when jealousy is creeping into your child's heart over someone else's praise or good fortune.
  • David Makes a Big Mistake · 2 Samuel 11
    Reach for this when your child is tempted by something that isn't theirs and needs help looking away early.

  • Building the Temple · 1 Kings 6

  • "I Am Doing a Great Work" · Ezra 1; 3–7; Nehemiah 2; 4–6; 8
    Reach for this when your child is being teased or distracted from a good task and needs to stay focused and finish.

  • Be Strong and Don't Give Up · 2 Chronicles 15

  • The Temple Is Finished! · Ezra 1; 3–7; Nehemiah 2; 4–6; 8
    Reach for this when your child feels discouraged partway through a hard task and wants to quit.
  • Wings Like the Eagles · Isaiah 40:28–31

  • No Honor at Home · Mark 6:1–6

    Reach for this when your child feels pressure to go along with the crowd instead of quietly doing what's right.
  • The Fourth Walking With Them · Daniel 3:19–27
    Reach for this when your child is asked to do something wrong and needs the courage to stand true.
  • Come, See a Man! · John 4:27–42
    Reach for this when your child is excited about Jesus and you want them to tell a friend, like the woman who left her water jar and ran.

Keeping Promises

    Reach for this when your child doubts they are remembered and needs to hear the Lord remembers His covenant and His people.

    Reach for this when your child faces something scary or a bad voice and needs to know God makes them strong.
  • Free to Choose · Moses 4:1–4
    Reach for this when your child is choosing between right and wrong and needs to see Jesus choose to follow the Father.

    Reach for this when your child feels too small for a big job and needs to hear the Lord helps the little ones be brave.
  • Enoch Teaches the People · Moses 6:37–47

    Reach for this when your child sees someone in need and needs the picture of grain left in the corners of a field.

Hope

Wisdom

Knowing what to do with what you know, and asking Heavenly Father when you don't. (18 stories)

Wonder

Letting yourself be amazed — the doorway the Spirit most often walks through. (43 stories)

  • Abraham Counts the Stars · Abraham 3:2–3, 11–14

Curiosity

Asking the next honest question and trusting that truth can hold it. (12 stories)

Questions parents ask

How do I teach my child a virtue like honesty or kindness?

Show it in a story, name it out loud, ask one simple question about it, and then share a time you lived it yourself. Short, calm and often works better than a long lesson.
