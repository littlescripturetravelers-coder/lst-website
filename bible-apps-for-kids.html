How to choose

  • Your faith tradition. Some apps are made for Latter-day Saint families, some for any Christian family, one for Catholic families.
  • Watching or reading together. Animated episodes are great for a child to watch; a read-aloud picture book is made for a parent and child on the couch or in bed.
  • Your child's age. Shorter stories in small words suit preschoolers; longer episodes and games suit older children.
  • Free or paid. Several of the best apps are completely free. Paid ones usually add something new every day or week.
  • Something for you, too. Some apps include a study for the parent; children learn the most when the grown-up is learning with them.

Bible App for Kids

Made by: Life.Church (YouVersion), in partnership with OneHope

The App Store listing says it is "always completely free" and offers "interactive adventures and beautiful animations", touch-activated animations, fun facts and activities, and challenges that earn rewards. OneHope describes it as bringing "41 major Bible stories to life through animation, narration, music, and sound effects" for "children ages 2-6", in 70 languages. Its website also offers free coloring pages, parent and church resources, and a printed companion, the Bible Storybook.

Best for: very young children of any Christian family who want a free, playful first look at the big Bible stories.

Sources: App Store · OneHope · bibleappforkids.com

Superbook Kids Bible

Made by: The Christian Broadcasting Network (CBN Animation)

Free on the App Store. The listing offers "68 full-length, FREE EPISODES" of the Superbook animated series, a full Bible with videos and interactive content inside it, "more than 20 fun Bible games", answers to hundreds of kids' questions, over 80 topics, and Quests (interactive devotionals). Superbook's site says episodes, Bibles and games can be downloaded for offline use and that the app is ad-free.

Best for: school-age children who love animated episodes and games, in any Christian family.

Sources: App Store · superbookkids.com

Gospel for Kids

Made by: The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

Free. The Church describes it as "a gospel learning app for children that brings together illustrated scripture stories, coloring books, sing-along songs, and more". Its Scripture Stories combine "beautiful artwork, abridged accounts from the scriptures, and dramatized narration"; there are over 200 scenes to color, songs including playlists for Come, Follow Me, and games to memorize the Articles of Faith, with new content added from time to time.

Best for: Latter-day Saint children who want the Church's own illustrated scripture stories, songs and coloring, free.

Sources: App Store · ChurchofJesusChrist.org

Gospel Library

Made by: The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

Free: the Church's official app for the scriptures, Come, Follow Me and other study materials. For 2026 the Church says "additional resources for teaching children — such as games, stories and activities — can be found under “Come, Follow Me” in the Gospel Library", and its illustrated Old Testament Stories are "accessible digitally in Gospel Library".

Best for: every Latter-day Saint family, as the home of the scriptures and the Church's own lessons.

Sources: App Store · Church Newsroom, 2026

We Believe (formerly the Come Follow Me app)

Made by: the We Believe Foundation, a Utah non-profit run by independent Church members

Free. Short daily devotionals in a "Scripture + Quote + Question" format, mostly following Come, Follow Me, that take "less than 5 minutes". For children, it says, "we pull together and organize for you the best resources from around the web". The listing notes it is not made or endorsed by the Church.

Best for: Latter-day Saint families who want a short daily family devotional, free.

Sources: App Store

RHH Lifted (The Red Headed Hostess)

Made by: The Red Headed Hostess LLC, independent

A paid membership: "$14.99 a month" or $144 a year on its website; the app is a reader for members. It offers weekly Come, Follow Me study for every age, including Interactive Story Time, activities for kids and teens, Table Talk, weekly videos and printable posters. The listing notes it is not made or endorsed by the Church.

Best for: Latter-day Saint families who want a complete, prepared weekly Come, Follow Me plan for every age.

Sources: App Store · theredheadedhostess.com

Abide

Made by: Guideposts

A Christian Bible meditation and sleep app, mainly for grown-ups. Its site lists a 7-day free trial, then "$39.99 per year" or $9.99 a month. Abide's blog recommends its kids' sleep meditations, such as The Littlest Lamb, for bedtime.

Best for: parents who want Bible meditation for themselves, with some bedtime stories to share with their children.

Sources: App Store · abide.com · Abide blog

Hallow

Made by: Hallow, Inc.

A Catholic prayer app. It is free to download, with a free version and an optional paid subscription whose price depends on your region. Its Kids & Families content includes prayers for different ages, "Bedtime Prayers" and stories such as Creation Story, saint stories and sacrament preparation; parents can set up kids profiles.

Best for: Catholic families who want prayer and bedtime stories for children alongside their own prayer life.

Sources: App Store · Hallow help: kids content · Hallow help: cost

Little Scripture Travelers (that's us)

A new scripture bedtime story every night for children ages 3 to 8, written in short, simple words and checked against the scriptures, in two editions: Latter-day Saint, following Come, Follow Me, and Christian, for families of any church. Each night's story is a picture book read aloud in a recorded voice, with each word lit as it is read (a parent can turn that off). Here is what we believe sets it apart:

  • Four Travelers who live the lesson. Beau, Scout, Willow and Wren are the same four little characters every night. Each one shows how tonight's scripture lesson fits into a child's own small day: at home, with a brother or sister, at school, when something feels hard.
  • Feelings, named and handled well. Every story teaches a feeling by name, how to notice it in your mind and body, and how to make a good choice with it, using the Five Stones: Spot, Name, Find, Fork and Calm. How the Five Stones work.
  • Your family's own stories. Parent prompts help you remember and tell your children your own stories: how tonight's scripture lesson showed up in your life, your testimony of it, and stories of your ancestors. You can save them in Family Stories, right beside the scriptures. Family history for kids.
  • A study for the parent, on the same lesson. Each morning the Morning Lantern gives the parent a short gospel study (Quick, Medium or Deep) on the same scripture the children hear that night, so a parent's own study and the children's lesson match the same day.
  • Closer parents and children. Put together, it is ten quiet minutes a night when a parent and child read, talk, share a family story and sing, building their relationship as well as their faith.
  • A large, growing library. 728 scripture stories are written for the Old and New Testament (both editions), and every one is in the app to read and hear. So far the stories from June through December of the Old Testament year have their pictures; the rest are words-only for now, with pictures being added. For Latter-day Saint families, the plan is about 1,440 stories by the end of 2029, adding the Book of Mormon and the Doctrine and Covenants.
  • Beyond the screen. Every month's stories are also paperback and Kindle books on Amazon, and every story, song and lullaby is a printable, with weekly charts, crosswords and quizzes.

Every night also has a song and ends with the Lantern Lullaby, and Family Stories, a private journal for each person, flashcards and a free Bible timeline are included.

What we don't do yet: we don't have games, and our animated videos are just beginning (the first two are made, with more to come). We're on the iPhone and the web. After 7 free days it is $6.99 a month or $49 a year.

Best for: a parent and a young child reading a short scripture story together at bedtime, learning to live it and to handle big feelings, with the parent's own study and family stories woven in. Read a whole night free, nothing to join.

Questions parents ask

What is the best free Bible app for kids?

Several strong apps are completely free. Bible App for Kids (Life.Church and OneHope) and Superbook Kids Bible (CBN) are free for any Christian family; Gospel for Kids and Gospel Library are free from The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. The best one depends on your child's age and your faith tradition.

What is a good scripture app for Latter-day Saint (LDS) kids?

The Church's own Gospel for Kids and Gospel Library apps are free. Independent options include We Believe (free daily devotionals), RHH Lifted (a paid weekly Come, Follow Me plan) and Little Scripture Travelers (a nightly Come, Follow Me bedtime story, read aloud, with a parent devotional).

What makes Little Scripture Travelers different?

Four consistent little characters, the Travelers, show children how to live each scripture lesson in their own day; every story names a feeling and shows how to make a good choice with it; parent prompts help parents tell their own stories, testimony and ancestors' stories; and parents get a morning study on the same scripture their children hear that night. It also comes as paperback books and printables.

Is there a Bible app that reads stories aloud at bedtime?

Bible App for Kids narrates its stories; Abide and Hallow have bedtime stories for children; Little Scripture Travelers reads a new scripture picture book aloud every night with each word lit as it is read, and ends with a lullaby.
