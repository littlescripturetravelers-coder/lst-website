✦ OUR STORY ✦

Born from bedtime — and a prayer

My name is Levi Lewis. In February 2026, I was laid off after 13 years. Resume after resume — local jobs, remote jobs, government jobs — anything that could keep us afloat. Meanwhile, at home, my wife and I had a bedtime routine with our three- and five-year-old daughters. But the gospel portion wasn't landing the way I hoped. The girls would squirm. Their eyes would drift.

I wanted something short. Something visual. Something the girls would beg for. Something that would help me share my testimony in a way they could actually feel.

That's when two prayers collided — the search for a business, and the search for a way to reach my daughters. And the answer was the same answer.

Little Scripture Travelers was born.

✦ Why a story, and why the same four travelers

A principle explained to a four-year-old tends to slide off. The same principle lived by a character they know sticks, because the child watches someone their own size make the choice and feel what comes of it.

That is why the cast never changes. Beau, Scout, Willow, and Wren show up every single night, so your child is not meeting strangers at bedtime — they are catching up with friends. The routine does the heavy lifting, and the story does the teaching, so you can simply read and be present.

There is a measurable side to this too. Researchers at Ohio State estimated that reading one picture book a day gives a child about 78,000 words of exposure a year, and that children from literacy-rich homes hear roughly 1.4 million more words before kindergarten than children who are never read to. (Logan et al., Ohio State University)

✦ Why the same song every night

A lullaby is not background music. It is a signal. Sung at the same point in the same order every night, it becomes something the body learns to answer before a single word of instruction is spoken — which is why bedtime resistance so often fades once the song is fixed.

Nothing to negotiate, nothing to announce. The song starts, and the room changes.

This is not just sentiment. In a study of 144 infants published in Nature Human Behaviour, babies relaxed measurably — slower heart rate and reduced arousal — when they heard lullabies, including lullabies from unfamiliar cultures in languages they had never heard. The calming appears to be built into what a lullaby is. (Bainbridge et al., Nature Human Behaviour)

✦ Why the three questions come at 5 AM

The three questions in A Parent's Heart are not a quiz for your child. They are for you, and they arrive early on purpose — so they can sit with you through the whole day.

  • 1.A moment from your own life when you lived what tonight's story teaches.
  • 2.What that principle actually feels like on a hard day — something your child can recognize in you.
  • 3.A grandparent or ancestor who lived it before you were born, and how their example still reaches your family.

You read them over breakfast and let them turn over quietly while you work, drive, and fold laundry. By the time the lights go low, you are not simply reading a story to your child — you are telling them about the time your grandmother held on, and the scripture story has become your family's story too.
